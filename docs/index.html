In DMD-distilled autoregressive video diffusion models, queries and keys remain aligned with their clean-source counterparts across denoising timesteps, while values diverge significantly: the attention routing is preserved, and changes in the generated content are primarily carried by the values. AR-Edit caches the source’s values from a single clean forward pass and injects them while each chunk is denoised from pure noise: real-time, structure-preserving streaming video editing, with no optimization, inversion or training.

Abstract

Editing a video stream in real time, without access to future frames, is essential for interactive applications. Existing training-free video editing methods, however, assume full offline access to the entire sequence, while streaming approaches either rely on additional training or are limited to specific editing techniques. We show that this limitation can be removed by analyzing the self-attention dynamics of DMD-distilled autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models. We uncover a key asymmetry: queries and keys remain stable and aligned with their source counterparts across denoising timesteps, while values diverge and encode the evolving content. Inherited from the non-distilled bidirectional teacher, this asymmetry is strongest at high noise, where few-step streaming models operate. The resulting mechanism is simple: a single forward pass suffices to capture source identity in the value features, which can be reused throughout generation. Starting from pure Gaussian noise and injecting only these source values reconstructs the input with fidelity matching or surpassing inversion-based methods, effectively eliminating inversion. Based on this insight, we introduce AR-Edit, a training-free, inversion-free method that caches source values once and selectively injects them during denoising. Across multiple AR backbones, AR-Edit enables real-time streaming editing with superior source preservation and tonal fidelity at minimal overhead.

Queries and keys stay close to their clean versions at every depth, even at t = 1000, where the input is pure noise; values do not. The attention routing (which tokens attend to which) is nearly invariant to the noise level, while the content read out through V is timestep-specific. This suggests a simple strategy: cache V from a single source forward pass at t = 0 and inject it during denoising. Because Q and K at any timestep are already structurally compatible with V at t = 0, the injected values slot into the attention computation without disrupting the routing that the target prompt governs through Q and K.

Switch to the Wan 2.1 teacher to see where this comes from. The non-distilled bidirectional model shows the same asymmetry at high noise, and it fades as t approaches 0. Few-step students are trained and sampled only at t ≥ 625 (Reward Forcing) or t ≥ 522 (CausVid), exactly where it is strongest.

Does inversion still matter?

V from t = 0 is structurally compatible with Q and K at any denoising timestep. This suggests that inversion may not be necessary with autoregressive video diffusion models.

We test this with six reconstruction recipes on our benchmark. Each recipe is defined by three choices: the initial noise zT, where the injected V tensors come from, and the timestep at which they are taken. Pick a recipe in the table to see what it gives back.

V taken at

Drawing zT from N(0, I) and injecting V from t = 0 obtained via a single forward pass of the source video (Ours) outperforms or matches every more expensive variant at a single pre-denoising NFE with neither inversion nor per-step matching of V.

Autoregressive video editing without inversion

For each incoming video chunk, a single forward pass caches the source V features. The edited output is then denoised from Gaussian noise conditioned on the target prompt, while the cached V is selectively injected.

The target Q and K guide the edit semantics, while the injected V preserves source identity. In later steps, a commit-then-anchor schedule confines the injection to the unedited region via a cross-attention mask, and a text boost emphasizes the new text tokens in cross-attention. The method requires no training or optimization and integrates with rolling KV caches for streaming. Select a pass to see which values the injected blocks read.

      AR-Edit exposes a small set of controls: how many steps inject source values in full (S), when the mask takes over (SCtA), which blocks receive the values (bmin) and which build the mask (bCtA), and how strongly new words are boosted (s). Everything not under study stays at its default.
